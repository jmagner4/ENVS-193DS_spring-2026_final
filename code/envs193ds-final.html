Problems

Problem 1. Research Writing

a.

In part 1, I think they used binomial/logistic regression. If habitat use is measured as used/not used, then the response variable would be binary. In part 2, I think they used a Spearman’s correlation test. Wanting to know the relationship between two variables suggests a correlation test should be used. Since both variables are categorical/nominal that suggests a non-parametric test should be used.

b.

Part 1: Our evidence suggests that the distance to the water’s edge is an important factor in determining where American Avocet choose to live.

Part 2: Different shorebird species, including American Avocet, Forster’s Tern, Black-necked Stilt, prefer different types of coastal habitats.

c.

One potential variable influencing the probability of American Avocet microhabitat use is the availability of food or prey species in the habitats as well; this population would be a discrete variable (measured as a number of individuals per one-meter net sweep). I expect American Avocets to choose areas with higher prey populations because it makes finding food and surviving easier.

b.

For the case_control column, a 1 represents a tree where a bird nest is present while a 0 represents an “available” tree where no birds nests are present.

c.

Tree height is a continuous variable. As tree height increases the probability of nest occurrence would increase according to paragraph 2 of the results section. It is unclear specifically why, but they do say tree architecture has an important role in nest site selection in the discussion; maybe because tall trees have less predators.

Acacia ant species is a categorical variable (confirmed in the methods section). The probability of finding a bird’s nest is higher for trees occupied by C. nigriceps compared to C. mimosae (found in second paragraph of results section).
